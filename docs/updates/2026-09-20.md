## Accepted items

### 1) RSV vaccination: large real-world effectiveness study in older adults

- **Citation / URL:** Lu Y, Smith ER, McEvoy R, et al. *Respiratory Syncytial Virus Vaccine Effectiveness Among US Older Adults, 2023-2024 Season.* JAMA Network Open. 2026 Sep 1. DOI: [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.34788](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.34788). PMID: [42758507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42758507/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Nationally representative retrospective cohort / postmarketing vaccine-effectiveness study.
- **Population:** US Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries aged ≥65; median age 74 years; 14.8 million initially unvaccinated beneficiaries.
- **Rubric score:** 9.0/10.
- **Summary:** During the 2023-2024 RSV season, vaccination was associated with high effectiveness against RSV-related hospitalization and RSV-related death in routine care.
- **Main finding:** Estimated vaccine effectiveness was 81.8% against RSV-related hospitalization and 85.0% against RSV-related death; effectiveness against hospitalization was 72.2% in immunocompromised individuals and did not vary significantly by age ≥65.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** The study median age matches the repository target age, and severe RSV prevention is directly relevant to infection resilience, hospitalization avoidance, and cardiopulmonary reserve in older adults.
- **Limitations:** Observational claims-based design; vaccinated people differed from unvaccinated people in socioeconomic and prior-vaccination characteristics; residual healthy-vaccinee bias and coding/misclassification are possible; results are product/season/context specific.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** RSV vaccination decisions should be discussed with a clinician using current guidance, prior vaccine history, immune status, cardiopulmonary disease, allergy history, and shared decision-making. This does not imply that every individual should be vaccinated outside guideline context.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes. Infection-resilience confidence for RSV vaccination in older adults was strengthened with a large Medicare cohort whose median age was 74.

### 2) Sedentary fragmentation, cardiovascular disease, mortality, and life expectancy

- **Citation / URL:** Xing T, Tian X, Liu J, Cheng X, et al. *Association of wearable-device-measured fragmented sedentary behavior with cardiovascular disease, mortality, and life expectancy.* Zhong Nan Da Xue Xue Bao Yi Xue Ban. 2026 Jul 28. DOI: [10.11817/j.issn.1672-7347.2026.260193](https://doi.org/10.11817/j.issn.1672-7347.2026.260193). PMID: [42763296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42763296/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Prospective cohort using wrist accelerometer data.
- **Population:** 89,010 UK Biobank participants; median age 63.5 years; 56.5% women; median follow-up 8.0 years.
- **Rubric score:** 8.2/10.
- **Summary:** The study examined total sedentary time and number of sedentary breaks in relation to mortality, incident cardiovascular disease, cardiovascular mortality, and estimated life expectancy.
- **Main finding:** Participants with ≥10 hours/day sedentary time and fewer than 12 sedentary breaks/day had higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.515), cardiovascular mortality (subdistribution HR 1.553), and incident CVD (HR 1.100) than the least sedentary group. Life-expectancy loss was mainly in CVD-free years, and more frequent sedentary breaks appeared to attenuate risk.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** This supports a practical, low-risk discussion point: not only total activity but also long uninterrupted sitting may matter for cardiovascular healthspan. It complements, rather than replaces, resistance/aerobic/balance training.
- **Limitations:** Observational design; UK Biobank is healthier and less representative than the general older population; accelerometer behavior was measured for only 3-7 days; causality and intervention effect are not proven; median age was younger than 74.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** Breaking up sitting is usually low risk, but people with falls risk, orthostatic symptoms, neuropathy, severe arthritis, or cardiac symptoms should individualize movement breaks with clinical/exercise-professional advice.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes, modestly. Added sedentary-time fragmentation as a cardiometabolic/function-preservation behavior with cohort-level support.

### 3) HbA1c versus fasting glucose for predicting cardiometabolic multimorbidity

- **Citation / URL:** Seidu S, Kunutsor SK, Khunti K. *Glycemic Markers and Cardiometabolic Multiple Long-Term Conditions: Comparative Roles of Glycated Hemoglobin and Fasting Plasma Glucose in the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing.* Mayo Clinic Proceedings: Innovations, Quality & Outcomes. 2026 Oct. DOI: [10.1016/j.mayocpiqo.2026.100756](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mayocpiqo.2026.100756). PMID: [42761864](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42761864/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Nationally representative longitudinal cohort analysis.
- **Population:** 2,233 adults in England; mean age 63; 45.2% men; free of hypertension, coronary disease, diabetes, and stroke at baseline; 12-15 years of follow-up.
- **Rubric score:** 7.8/10.
- **Summary:** Baseline HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose were compared as predictors of later cardiometabolic multiple long-term conditions.
- **Main finding:** Both markers predicted incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity, but HbA1c was the stronger and more informative marker after adjustment; per 1% HbA1c increase, OR 1.99 for incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** Supports standard, clinician-led cardiometabolic risk tracking and reinforces HbA1c as a useful longitudinal marker, not merely a diabetes diagnostic number.
- **Limitations:** Observational; modest number of incident multimorbidity cases; baseline cohort was younger than 74; results do not define individualized treatment thresholds.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** HbA1c interpretation can be distorted by anemia, kidney disease, altered red-cell turnover, hemoglobin variants, and some medications. Medication or diet changes should be clinician-guided.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor. Reinforces standard labs/risk-factor tracking rather than adding a new intervention.

### 4) NAD+ concentration and frailty: useful caution for NAD-boosting claims

- **Citation / URL:** Shida T, Hatanaka S, Kojima N, Osuka Y, Sasai H, et al. *Association between whole-blood NAD+ concentration and frailty in community-dwelling older adults: the Itabashi Longitudinal Study on Aging.* GeroScience. 2026 Sep 16. DOI: [10.1007/s11357-026-02545-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02545-9). PMID: [42744962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42744962/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Cross-sectional community cohort / biomarker association study.
- **Population:** 529 community-dwelling adults aged ≥65 in Japan; 32 participants had frailty.
- **Rubric score:** 7.5/10 as a watchlist/caution item, not as intervention evidence.
- **Summary:** Higher whole-blood NAD+ concentration was associated with lower odds of frailty, but the association weakened after adjustment for hematocrit.
- **Main finding:** Each 1 µmol/L higher whole-blood NAD+ was associated with lower frailty odds in basic models (OR 0.871), but adjustment for blood cell composition attenuated the association.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** The age group is relevant, and frailty is a high-priority outcome. The study is more useful for interpreting NAD biomarker claims than for choosing supplements.
- **Limitations:** Cross-sectional; only 32 frail participants; whole-blood NAD+ may reflect blood cell composition; no supplement intervention and no clinical outcome follow-up.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** This should not be interpreted as evidence to start NAD precursors. Supplements can vary in dose/quality and may interact with comorbidities, cancer biology questions, liver/kidney disease, or medications.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes for the watchlist. NAD+ remains early; this adds biomarker-association caution rather than confidence in NAD-boosting interventions.

### 5) Smoking cessation and epigenetic clocks: biomarker support for a standard risk-reduction priority

- **Citation / URL:** Kim Y, Kang H. *Duration of smoking cessation and DNA methylation aging clocks among US adults: potential reversibility of smoking-induced biological aging.* GeroScience. 2026 Sep 15. DOI: [10.1007/s11357-026-02535-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02535-x). PMID: [42736515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42736515/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Cross-sectional analysis of NHANES DNA methylation data.
- **Population:** 1,911 US adults aged 50-84.
- **Rubric score:** 7.6/10.
- **Summary:** Longer time since smoking cessation was associated with lower epigenetic age acceleration across multiple methylation clocks.
- **Main finding:** Current smokers had the highest epigenetic age acceleration; former smokers with ≥20 years of cessation approached never-smoker levels for GrimAge2, with dose-response patterns across cessation duration.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** If smoking history is relevant, this reinforces smoking cessation as a conventional healthspan priority. It also helps interpret epigenetic-clock changes as supportive biomarkers rather than stand-alone treatment targets.
- **Limitations:** Cross-sectional; residual confounding; smoking exposure and cessation history may be imperfect; epigenetic clocks are not validated as treatment-directing clinical endpoints.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** Smoking-cessation pharmacotherapy or nicotine replacement should be individualized with a clinician/pharmacist, especially with cardiovascular disease, psychiatric history, or polypharmacy.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor. Reinforces conventional smoking-risk reduction and the repository’s caution that epigenetic clocks are not treatment-directing.

### 6) Late-life depressive symptoms and dementia/Alzheimer mortality

- **Citation / URL:** Benito-León J, Serrano JI, Del Castillo MD, Escolà-Gascón Á, et al. *Population-based cohort study of modifiable risk factors for dementia and Alzheimer disease mortality in Spain.* Communications Health. 2026. DOI: [10.1038/s44528-026-00022-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44528-026-00022-5). PMID: [42757362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42757362/).
- **Date found:** 2026-09-20.
- **Evidence type:** Population-based prospective cohort with competing-risk models.
- **Population:** 3,085 older adults in the NEDICES cohort, enrolled 1994-1995 and followed through 2017.
- **Rubric score:** 7.7/10.
- **Summary:** The study assessed late-life modifiable factors in relation to dementia-coded and Alzheimer-disease-coded mortality.
- **Main finding:** Depressive symptoms were the clearest modifiable correlate, especially for AD-coded mortality and particularly among women; several inverse associations for smoking, obesity, and hypertension likely reflected competing mortality and late-life reverse causation.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** Supports treating mood symptoms as part of cognitive-aging and whole-health risk assessment, while not overinterpreting dementia-mortality coding as dementia-incidence prevention evidence.
- **Limitations:** Mortality-certificate outcomes miss nonfatal dementia; older baseline era; sex-specific signal was stronger in women; observational associations can reflect reverse causation and competing risk.
- **Safety / clinician cautions:** Depressive symptoms warrant clinician discussion and standard mental-health assessment. This does not identify a specific antidepressant, supplement, or dementia-prevention treatment.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor. Reinforces multidomain cognitive-health attention to mood, sleep, vascular risk, function, and social context.

## Evidence-map / watchlist disposition

- RSV vaccination confidence for severe-outcome prevention in older adults was strengthened.
- Sedentary-time fragmentation was added as a practical cardiometabolic/function-preservation behavior supported by large cohort evidence.
- NAD+ remains a cautionary biomarker/watchlist area; the new frailty association does **not** establish supplement benefit.
- Epigenetic-clock evidence continues to be treated as supportive/early and not treatment-directing.
