## Included items

### 1) Eccentric-overload training for lower-limb strength and power in older adults

- **Citation:** Lan C, Lei B, Huang W, et al. *Effects of eccentric overload training on lower-limb muscle strength, gait performance, balance, and explosive power in older adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* Experimental Gerontology. 2026 Aug 27. PMID: [42660338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42660338/). DOI: [10.1016/j.exger.2026.113305](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2026.113305).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
- **Population:** Older adults; 15 RCTs, 438 participants.
- **Rubric score:** 8.2/10 — highly relevant to sarcopenia/function preservation, randomized evidence, and clinically meaningful strength/power outcomes; limited by small trial sizes and protocol heterogeneity.
- **Summary:** Eccentric-overload training was associated with better lower-limb strength and explosive power. Effects were clearer versus inactive controls, while comparisons with conventional resistance training suggested possible non-inferiority rather than decisive superiority.
- **Main finding:** Pooled effects favored eccentric-overload training for lower-limb strength (SMD 0.50, 95% CI 0.12–0.87) and explosive power (SMD 0.39, 95% CI 0.07–0.71). Longer programs (≥8 weeks) appeared more effective; flywheel-based approaches ranked favorably in exploratory subgroup analyses.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** Adds nuance to the exercise map: power and eccentric-loading strategies may be useful clinician/physiotherapist discussion topics for preserving stairs, gait reserve, and fall-recovery capacity, but they should be progressed safely rather than treated as a do-it-yourself anti-aging shortcut.
- **Limitations:** Small total sample; varying equipment, supervision, frequency, and comparator groups; adverse-event reporting may be incomplete; functional outcomes beyond strength/power remain less certain.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice. Eccentric overload can cause delayed soreness and may aggravate tendons/joints if progressed too quickly. Discuss with a clinician or exercise professional if there is osteoporosis, prior tendon injury, severe arthritis, neuropathy, balance impairment, cardiovascular symptoms, or recent falls.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes — strengthens the power-training/eccentric-loading nuance under exercise and function without replacing conventional progressive resistance and balance training.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes, especially for supervised progression and fall-risk context.

### 2) Frailty predicts ADL dependence, mortality, discharge, and readmission after hospitalization

- **Citation:** Kamo T, Asahi R, Inaba Y, et al. *Impact of Frailty on Activities of Daily Living in Hospitalized Older Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* Geriatrics & Gerontology International. 2026 Sep. PMID: [42642794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42642794/). DOI: [10.1111/ggi.70811](https://doi.org/10.1111/ggi.70811).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies.
- **Population:** Hospitalized adults aged ≥65 years or samples predominantly ≥65; 57 cohort studies.
- **Rubric score:** 8.0/10 — directly relevant to older-adult resilience and independence; large evidence base and clinically meaningful outcomes; observational design and substantial heterogeneity limit causal interpretation.
- **Summary:** Frailty status in hospitalized older adults was associated with worse ADL status, higher mortality, lower likelihood of discharge home, and higher short-term readmission risk.
- **Main finding:** Frail patients had higher mortality (RR 2.26, 95% CI 1.86–2.76), lower likelihood of discharge home (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.73–0.86), and higher 30-day and 90-day readmission risk.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** Reinforces frailty and ADL function as practical resilience metrics before, during, and after hospital episodes. For an older man, prehabilitation, medication review, nutrition, mobility planning, and discharge support are clinician-discussion issues, not just “fitness” topics.
- **Limitations:** Observational cohorts; substantial heterogeneity; different frailty instruments and hospital contexts; residual confounding likely.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice. Hospital or post-hospital frailty should prompt clinician-led assessment rather than unsupervised aggressive exercise. Watch for delirium risk, deconditioning, undernutrition, orthostasis, sedating medications, and fall hazards.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor — strengthens the existing frailty-screening and multidomain-support stance as risk stratification for hospitalization resilience.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes, particularly around prehabilitation, discharge planning, and post-hospital rehabilitation.

### 3) Influenza vaccination and severe outcomes in hospitalized adults aged 65+

- **Citation:** Marques DFP, Rose AMC, Monge S, et al. *Association between influenza vaccination and severe outcomes in adults aged ≥65 years hospitalized with laboratory-confirmed influenza: a retrospective cohort study, I-MOVE hospital network, Europe, 2015/16–2024/25.* Expert Review of Vaccines. 2026 Dec. PMID: [42657682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42657682/). DOI: [10.1080/14760584.2026.2722420](https://doi.org/10.1080/14760584.2026.2722420).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Multisite retrospective cohort study.
- **Population:** 4,257 adults aged ≥65 years hospitalized with laboratory-confirmed influenza in the European I-MOVE hospital network, 2015/16–2024/25 seasons.
- **Rubric score:** 8.0/10 — highly relevant to infection resilience in older adults and uses clinically meaningful outcomes; limited by observational design and hospitalized-case restriction.
- **Summary:** Among older adults hospitalized with confirmed influenza, prior vaccination was associated with lower in-hospital death, while the association with ICU admission was uncertain.
- **Main finding:** Vaccination was associated with a 26% relative risk reduction in in-hospital death (95% CI 3–43). Estimated reduction in ICU admission was smaller and statistically uncertain overall.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** Reinforces age-appropriate seasonal vaccination as an infection-resilience and severe-outcome prevention topic, especially because mortality/hospitalization outcomes matter more for longevity than antibody or biomarker endpoints.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective design; residual healthy-vaccinee or frailty confounding possible; only hospitalized influenza cases were studied; vaccine formulation, season, and viral match vary.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice. Vaccine decisions should follow current clinician/public-health guidance and individual contraindications, prior reactions, immunocompromise, timing, and product availability.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes — modestly updates the infection-resilience entry with severe in-hospital outcome evidence in adults ≥65.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes during seasonal vaccine planning, especially when comorbidities or prior adverse reactions are present.

### 4) Semaglutide and serious infections in type 2 diabetes plus chronic kidney disease

- **Citation:** Rayner B, Mahaffey KW, Mann JFE, et al. *Effect of semaglutide on COVID-19 and other infections: an analysis from the FLOW randomized clinical trial.* Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation. 2026 Aug 27. PMID: [41728915](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41728915/). DOI: [10.1093/ndt/gfag036](https://doi.org/10.1093/ndt/gfag036).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Prespecified analysis from a randomized placebo-controlled trial.
- **Population:** Participants with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease in the FLOW trial.
- **Rubric score:** 8.1/10 for disease-specific watchlist relevance — randomized data and clinically meaningful serious-infection/hospitalization outcomes; applicability is narrow to T2D+CKD and semaglutide 1.0 mg.
- **Summary:** Semaglutide reduced a composite of first infection serious adverse event, hospitalization due to infection, or all-cause death versus placebo.
- **Main finding:** The main composite hazard ratio was 0.79 (95% CI 0.69–0.89). Infection serious adverse events, COVID-19 serious adverse events, COVID-19 adverse events, and infection hospitalization were each lower in the semaglutide group.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** If an older man has type 2 diabetes and CKD, this adds disease-specific evidence that GLP-1 therapy may affect infection-related outcomes as part of broader cardiorenal risk management. It does not imply semaglutide is a general immune-resilience or longevity drug for people without standard indications.
- **Limitations:** Secondary/prespecified analysis rather than the primary FLOW endpoint; high-risk T2D+CKD population; cannot cleanly separate weight, glycemia, kidney, cardiovascular, and infection pathways; not a trial of healthy aging.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice and not a recommendation to start or stop semaglutide. Discuss indication, kidney function, gastrointestinal effects, dehydration, nutrition/lean-mass monitoring, retinopathy/ocular concerns, gallbladder/pancreatitis history, and medication interactions with a clinician.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor — refines GLP-1 watchlist language for indicated T2D+CKD contexts and infection outcomes.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes only if diabetes/CKD/obesity/cardiorenal indications are present.

### 5) Dapagliflozin after TAVI for severe aortic stenosis

- **Citation:** Raposeiras Roubín S, Gonzalez-Manzanares R, Amat-Santos I, et al. *Treatment effects of dapagliflozin in patients with aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation across left ventricular ejection fraction.* European Journal of Heart Failure. 2026 Aug 27. PMID: [42655906](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42655906/). DOI: [10.1093/ejhf/xuag242](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejhf/xuag242).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Pragmatic multicenter randomized open-label trial with blinded endpoint adjudication.
- **Population:** Elderly patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI); 1,223 with baseline LVEF data in this analysis.
- **Rubric score:** 8.0/10 for indicated cardiovascular care — older population, randomized design, and hard clinical composite; relevance depends on having severe aortic stenosis/TAVI context.
- **Summary:** Dapagliflozin reduced all-cause death or worsening heart failure after TAVI consistently across left-ventricular ejection-fraction subgroups.
- **Main finding:** The treatment effect did not significantly differ by baseline LVEF; dapagliflozin was generally well tolerated, but genitourinary infections were more frequent.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** For an older man facing or recovering from TAVI, this is a clinician-discussion item about SGLT2 inhibitor use in a specific cardiovascular setting. It does not support SGLT2 inhibitors as general longevity drugs in people without standard indications.
- **Limitations:** Subgroup/secondary analysis across LVEF; open-label pragmatic design; 1-year follow-up; Spanish TAVI population; not generalizable to people without severe aortic stenosis or heart-failure risk.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice. SGLT2 inhibitors require clinician monitoring for volume depletion, orthostatic symptoms, kidney function, genital/urinary infections, ketoacidosis risk, and perioperative/sick-day management.
- **Evidence-map change:** Minor — adds TAVI/severe-aortic-stenosis context to SGLT2 disease-specific evidence.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes only in relevant cardiology/cardiorenal contexts.

### 6) GLP-1 receptor agonists and incident cancer signal in type 2 diabetes claims data

- **Citation:** Sklepinski SM, Herrin J, Swarna KS, et al. *Association of GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, and sulfonylureas with incident cancer in type 2 diabetes.* Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice. 2026 Sep. PMID: [42607727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42607727/). DOI: [10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113503](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113503).
- **Date found:** 2026-08-30.
- **Evidence type:** Observational target-trial emulation using claims data.
- **Population:** Adults with type 2 diabetes and moderate cardiovascular risk initiating GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, or sulfonylureas; more than 350,000 initiators across Optum Labs and Medicare data.
- **Rubric score:** 7.6/10 as a cautionary watchlist item — large real-world dataset and safety relevance, but observational comparisons are vulnerable to residual confounding, surveillance bias, and drug-channeling effects.
- **Summary:** GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation was associated with higher new cancer diagnosis risk compared with DPP-4 inhibitors, while SGLT2 inhibitors and sulfonylureas had lower risk compared with GLP-1 receptor agonists. The authors reported that results were driven by obesity-nonrelated cancers.
- **Main finding:** GLP-1 receptor agonists versus DPP-4 inhibitors: HR 1.13 (95% CI 1.05–1.21) for new cancer diagnosis. The authors called for further investigation and caution among at-risk individuals.
- **Relevance to a 74-year-old male:** This does not prove GLP-1 drugs cause cancer, but it is relevant to clinician-led risk/benefit discussion in older adults because baseline cancer risk rises with age and GLP-1 use is expanding.
- **Limitations:** Nonrandomized claims-data analysis; cancer ascertainment may reflect differential screening or healthcare contact; confounding by obesity severity, diabetes trajectory, and prescribing channel is possible; not a longevity trial.
- **Safety/clinician cautions:** Not medical advice and not a recommendation to stop clinically indicated therapy. Discuss personal cancer history, screening status, diabetes/obesity/cardiorenal indication strength, and alternatives with a clinician.
- **Evidence-map change:** Yes — adds a watchlist caution that GLP-1 risk/benefit discussions should not focus only on weight, muscle, kidney, or ocular signals.
- **Clinician discussion warranted:** Yes when GLP-1 therapy is being considered or monitored.

## Overall evidence-map disposition

This update strengthens confidence in supervised, individualized exercise programming as the central healthspan platform: eccentric/power-oriented resistance methods may be useful adjuncts, while frailty assessment remains important for hospital resilience and recovery planning. Infection resilience is reinforced by older-adult influenza data with in-hospital mortality relevance. Cardiometabolic drug findings remain disease-specific: semaglutide and dapagliflozin findings are important for indicated T2D/CKD/TAVI contexts, not general longevity use. The GLP-1 cancer signal is a cautionary watchlist item requiring clinician interpretation rather than an automated treatment recommendation.
