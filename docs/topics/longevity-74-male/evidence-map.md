# Evidence Map

## Legend

- **Strong**: multiple human RCTs/meta-analyses, clinically meaningful outcomes
- **Moderate**: some human trial evidence or strong observational evidence
- **Early**: plausible but limited human evidence
- **Speculative**: mostly animal/mechanistic, commercial hype, or unclear endpoints

## Intervention categories

### Exercise and function

- Resistance training: **Strong; newer RCT meta-analysis evidence supports resistance exercise as the clearest intervention for improving muscle-specific strength in adults ≥60, with nutrition and aerobic training still important but not substitutes for progressive loading. A 2026 meta-analysis of blood-flow-restriction resistance training in older adults found selected strength and quadriceps-size gains versus no exercise, but mixed comparisons with conventional training and acute blood-pressure elevations; it is a supervised alternative for selected people rather than a superior or cardiovascularly safer default. A frail/prefrail-specific 2026 meta-analysis of nine RCTs found possible knee-extensor, grip-strength, and gait-speed gains, but low-to-very-low certainty and uncertain effects on broader performance and frailty scores**
- Aerobic training / VO₂ max: **Strong; a 2026 pragmatic RCT in community-dwelling adults ≥60 found that a 12-month structured exercise program reduced epicardial adipose volume, adding cardiometabolic-imaging support while still relying on clinical supervision and individualized progression for older adults with symptoms or fall/orthopedic risks**
- Reducing prolonged uninterrupted sitting: **Moderate observational evidence; a 2026 UK Biobank wearable cohort of 89,010 participants linked ≥10 h/day sedentary time plus low sedentary-break frequency with higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality and fewer CVD-free years, supporting practical movement-break discussions while preserving causality and falls-risk cautions**
- Balance and fall prevention: **Strong for improving balance/physical-function measures and for exercise-centered fall prevention; a large 2026 Bayesian network meta-analysis of 423 RCTs in adults ≥60 supports exercise for balance and adds modality-specific nuance, while a 2026 frailty-focused meta-analysis shows short-term gait/TUG/sit-to-stand gains but limited direct fall-incidence evidence in physically frail adults. A 2026 Cochrane update of 110 RCTs in community-dwelling adults ≥60 found individualized multifactorial programs may reduce fall rate and recurrent falls versus usual care, but effects versus exercise or falls education are uncertain or small. The 2026 eSTEPS cluster RCT in 11,945 primary-care patients with mean age 77 found that electronic-workflow support for tailored exercise and optional physical-therapy referral modestly reduced documented falls and fall-related injuries, strengthening the implementation case while showing that uptake and absolute effects remain limited. A 2026 network meta-analysis of 60 randomized trials found telerehabilitation and exergaming improved some balance outcomes versus no treatment but did not outperform conventional exercise; no included trial reported falls, so digital delivery should not be mistaken for direct fall-prevention evidence**
- Power training for older adults: **Moderate; a 2026 RCT meta-analysis suggests eccentric-overload training can improve lower-limb strength and explosive power in older adults, but small trials, equipment needs, soreness/tendon risk, and heterogeneous protocols make clinician/exercise-professional supervision important**
- Multidomain frailty interventions combining home/community exercise with nutrition, cognitive, psychological, social, environmental, and daily-function supports: **Moderate; a 2026 network meta-analysis of 22 RCTs in community-dwelling prefrail/frail older adults ranked exercise+cognitive approaches highly for frailty reduction and exercise+nutrition+psychological approaches highly for motor ability, while preserving heterogeneity/ranking uncertainty. Large harmonized cohort evidence also suggests that frailty trajectory can dominate BMI category for stroke-risk stratification, reinforcing functional assessment alongside cardiometabolic assessment**
- Multidomain lifestyle intervention for cognitive-risk older adults: **Moderate; LatAm-FINGERS and SUPERBRAIN-MEET support structured exercise/diet/cognitive-social/vascular-risk programs for cognitive composite outcomes in at-risk older adults. A 2026 dose-response network meta-analysis of 38 randomized trials in adults ≥60 with mild cognitive impairment suggests executive-function gains are most reliable at moderate exercise doses (roughly 300–1,000 MET-min/week), but certainty is low to moderate, indirect/model-based comparisons are important, and dementia-incidence, independence, and implementation evidence remain developing**

### Nutrition and body composition

- Adequate protein intake: **Moderate to Strong; a 2026 preregistered systematic review spanning 248 studies found the most consistent support for protein/leucine-centered strategies paired with resistance training, while emphasizing heterogeneous sarcopenia definitions and outcomes. A newer network meta-analysis of 57 RCTs in sarcopenia/presarcopenia ranked combined exercise and nutrition highest for selected strength and muscle-mass outcomes, while exercise was more consistent for physical performance; overall network certainty was limited. Protein and nutrition targets require individualization for kidney function, total diet, appetite/weight loss, and other clinical factors**
- Mediterranean-style dietary pattern: **Strong**
- Creatine for older adults: **Moderate**
- Aggressive calorie restriction or intentional weight loss in older adults: **Risk-dependent; caution; LOSE-AF suggests supervised weight loss in older adults with overweight and persistent AF can be feasible without trial-related serious safety concerns, but it did not improve AF symptoms/burden over 8 months and lean-mass/frailty risks still need individualized monitoring**

### Sleep and recovery

- Exercise for subjective sleep quality: **Moderate; a 2026 network meta-analysis of 39 studies and 3,198 adults aged ≥60 found that balance, aerobic, multicomponent, and resistance activity improved Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores versus control. Exercise-type rankings relied partly on indirect comparisons and do not establish balance training as the best insomnia treatment. Exercise can complement but should not replace evaluation of sleep apnea, persistent insomnia, restless legs, nocturia, pain, mood, medication effects, or circadian disruption**

### Cardiometabolic risk

- Blood pressure control: **Strong; exercise is a core non-pharmacological adjunct, and a 2026 RCT network/dose-response meta-analysis in adults ≥45 found BP reductions across modalities, with combined/circuit aerobic-resistance approaches ranking favorably but not definitively superior. A 2026 Seniors-ENRICA-2 cohort in adults ≥65 linked higher Life’s Essential 8 cardiovascular-health scores with lower deficit accumulation and slower unhealthy-aging progression. The 2026 Taiwan multisociety older-adult hypertension guideline further emphasizes validated standardized measurement, out-of-office monitoring, individualized targets, orthostatic/frailty assessment, and avoidance of overtreatment**
- ApoB/LDL-C lowering when indicated: **Strong**
- Diabetes prevention/management: **Strong; long-term DPP/DPPOS follow-up supports intensive lifestyle intervention for lower multimorbidity burden in adults with prediabetes, whereas metformin did not show a comparable multimorbidity association in that analysis**
- SGLT2 inhibitors: **Strong for indicated type 2 diabetes/heart failure/chronic kidney disease contexts; older-adult randomized-subgroup meta-analysis in non-dialysis CKD supports renal and heart-failure benefit while reinforcing monitoring for genital infections and volume depletion/hypotension; emerging observational data suggest no excess geriatric-syndrome signal in older adults with type 2 diabetes, newer absolute-benefit models may help clinician-led targeting for primary heart-failure prevention in type 2 diabetes, and DapaTAVI extends disease-specific evidence to selected elderly patients after TAVI for severe aortic stenosis. A 2026 post hoc DapaTAVI analysis suggested greater relative benefit in frail than prefrail/robust participants, but small subgroups and interaction uncertainty make this indication-specific rather than evidence for general anti-aging use. A 2026 active-comparator cohort of adults aged ≥75 with type 2 diabetes found no detected increase in infection, ketoacidosis, falls, or fractures versus DPP-4 inhibitors and lower acute-kidney-injury and hospitalization rates, adding reassuring age-specific safety evidence while leaving uncommon outcomes imprecise and standard geriatric precautions unchanged**
- GLP-1 receptor agonists: **Strong for indicated obesity/diabetes contexts, with stronger disease-specific cardiorenal evidence in CKD/type 2 diabetes/ASCVD-risk contexts; monitor muscle/frailty implications and evolving safety signals including rare ocular events, mixed musculoskeletal/fall-fracture data, and unresolved cancer-signal surveillance. A 2026 age-stratified meta-analysis of eight cardiovascular-outcome trials (71,926 participants) found MACE reduction both below and above age 65 with no clear age interaction, although ≥75 data came from only four trials and remained comparatively sparse. A 2026 body-composition meta-analysis suggests adiposity reduction is plausible but muscle quantity/quality evidence remains low-certainty. A 2026 systematic review of eight small randomized trials found very-low-certainty, agent-specific skeletal evidence; semaglutide and weight-management-dose liraglutide trials with substantial weight loss reported reductions at some BMD sites, requiring fracture-oriented study rather than class-wide inference. FLOW infection analyses are encouraging only for high-risk T2D+CKD contexts, so older-adult monitoring should include indication strength, nutrition, strength, physical function, hydration, fracture risk, and routine cancer screening rather than body weight alone**

### Medication safety and polypharmacy

- Structured medication review / deprescribing conversations: **Strong as a safety practice; a 2026 BMJ population-level cohort of 2.3 million adults ≥66 prioritized 24 potentially inappropriate prescribing cascades, supporting clinician/pharmacist review of whether new symptoms may be medication adverse effects before adding another drug. A 2026 primary-care systematic review found more consistent improvements in prescribing appropriateness/adherence than in falls, function, mortality, healthcare use, or person-centered outcomes, so medication review should not be oversold as independently proven to extend healthspan**
- Statin discontinuation in later life: **Indication-, frailty-, and goals-dependent; a 2026 English population-wide cohort with mean age 75.9 found discontinuation associated with more all-cause hospitalization overall, but the association diminished with increasing frailty and was not higher in severe frailty or dementia. Care-home findings may reflect strong selection and prognosis effects. Chronological age alone is not a sufficient reason to stop, while observational evidence cannot determine an individual's best decision**
- Low-dose aspirin for general healthy aging/function preservation: **Not supported; a 2026 secondary analysis of 18,581 ASPREE participants found no preservation of multidomain intrinsic capacity, including no benefit in males. Aspirin remains an indication-specific medicine whose cardiovascular/cerebrovascular benefits must be weighed against bleeding and other risks with a clinician, not a general anti-inflammatory longevity intervention**

### Geroscience compounds

- Rapamycin/rapalogs: **Early for longevity; stronger in specific disease contexts. A 2026 propensity-weighted US transplant-registry cohort found no meaningful survival-age difference between 4,885 kidney-transplant recipients prescribed rapamycin and 6,589 not prescribed it over a median 11 years. This disease-specific observational null result strengthens caution but cannot answer whether different doses or schedules help generally healthy older adults**
- Metformin for longevity in non-diabetics: **Early/uncertain; a 2026 proof-of-concept randomized study in 40 older adults with insulin resistance reported preservation of selected brain imaging measures and a processing-speed/working-memory composite over 40 weeks, but the sample was small, outcomes were surrogate-heavy, and dementia, independence, quality of life, and survival were not tested**
- NAD precursors / NAD-boosting formulations: **Early; biomarker effects do not prove outcome benefit; a short phase 0/1b LNAD+ trial showed rapid intracellular NAD increases over 5 days without clinical endpoint improvement, and a 2026 Itabashi older-adult cross-sectional study linked lower whole-blood NAD+ with frailty but found attenuation after hematocrit adjustment, reinforcing biomarker-only caution. A 2026 narrative review of IV NAD+ / IV NAD+ precursor wellness use found sparse human evidence, mostly small uncontrolled studies/observational reports/case reports, short follow-up, uncertain clinical significance, and adverse effects such as nausea, cramping, flushing, and chest discomfort, so IV NAD+ remains a cautionary watchlist topic rather than an evidence-based longevity intervention**
- Senolytics: **Early/speculative outside trials**

### Diagnostics and tracking

- Standard labs and risk-factor tracking: **Strong**
- Handgrip strength, gait speed, and simple physical-performance measures as risk markers: **Moderate to Strong for risk stratification; not treatment-directing by themselves; a 2026 LifeAfter90 cohort found faster 4-meter gait speed associated with lower mortality risk in ethnoracially diverse adults aged ≥90, reinforcing gait speed as a functional vital sign while preserving observational-causality limits**
- Intrinsic-capacity / multidomain geriatric assessment concepts: **Moderate for risk stratification; implementation should be clinician-guided; newer Health and Retirement Study evidence suggests dynamic deterioration or recovery in intrinsic capacity tracks later cognitive decline and dementia risk, and newer BMI-frailty trajectory data suggest functional frailty meaningfully modifies long-term stroke risk. A 2026 CHARLS analysis in adults with early cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic risk found worsening frailty trajectories associated with higher CVD and stroke occurrence, supporting repeated frailty/function assessment alongside standard cardiometabolic markers. A 2026 Chinese Longitudinal Healthy Longevity Survey cohort found intrinsic capacity and frailty index jointly identified disability and mortality risk, while a 2026 Rugao Longevity and Aging Study cohort linked elevated/persistent BNP with intrinsic-capacity decline. Two independent Japanese cohorts of adults aged 85-89 also linked higher intrinsic capacity with lower mortality and selected disability outcomes, strengthening population-level risk-marker evidence while not establishing individual prediction or a consumer treatment target**
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM): **Strong for selected insulin-treated type 2 diabetes contexts; not a general longevity tracker**
- DEXA for bone/body composition: **Moderate to Strong depending on indication; post-fall hospitalization and fragility-fracture cohorts reinforce clinician-led bone-health evaluation after serious falls or low-trauma fractures, including in older men. A 2026 age-matched Taiwanese DXA cohort found older men had much lower DXA-defined osteoporosis prevalence than women but comparable vertebral and hip fracture prevalence, reinforcing that T-scores should be integrated with prior fractures, falls, medications, and other clinical risks. A 2026 BMJ network meta-analysis of 124 randomized exercise trials supports weight-bearing aerobic activity and combined aerobic-resistance exercise for BMD preservation at roughly 600 MET-min/week, while weighted-vest exercise has smaller RCT meta-analysis support versus usual activity. A small 2026 RCT in people with osteoporotic vertebral fracture found supervised spinal-extensor, postural, lower-extremity, and balance exercise improved pain and walking capacity, supporting tailored rehabilitation without proving recurrent-fracture prevention. Exercise remains an adjunct rather than a substitute for indicated osteoporosis care**
- Cognitive resilience and Alzheimer’s biomarker context: **Moderate for risk framing, not self-directed treatment; a 2026 Nature Medicine longitudinal cohort found that biomarker pathology and cognitive resilience independently and jointly predicted incident Alzheimer’s dementia, reinforcing attention to modifiable resilience domains while preserving caution that resilience scores and blood biomarkers are not stand-alone treatment-directing tools**
- Epigenetic clocks: **Early; not yet treatment-directing; recent cohort evidence suggests conventional risk factors can outperform epigenetic clocks for chronic-disease prediction, muscle-aging associations remain modest/sex-specific, smoking-cessation duration tracks more favorable methylation-aging profiles in NHANES, and 2026 reliability work shows biological instability can limit interpretation even when technical reproducibility is high**
- Polygenic longevity scores: **Early for personal decision-making; a Finnish cohort found modest mortality association, but smoking behavior and conventional risk factors were more practically important predictors and scores are not treatment-directing**
- Consumer biological age panels: **Use cautiously**

### Infection resilience

- Age-appropriate vaccination (for example influenza, COVID-19, RSV, pneumococcal, shingles per current clinical guidance): **Strong for preventing infection-related morbidity in indicated older adults; a 2026 US Medicare cohort with median age 74 found RSV vaccination effectiveness of 81.8% against RSV-related hospitalization and 85.0% against RSV-related death in the 2023-2024 season, adding to real-world adjuvanted RSVPreF3 effectiveness evidence against hospitalization/complications in adults ≥60. 2025/26 Stockholm data support seasonal influenza vaccine protection against hospitalization in adults ≥65, and I-MOVE hospitalized-case data link influenza vaccination with lower in-hospital death among adults ≥65. A 2026 clinical synthesis reported pooled European pragmatic-trial evidence favoring high-dose over standard-dose influenza vaccine for laboratory-confirmed influenza hospitalization in adults ≥65; enhanced-formulation preferences remain product-, season-, and jurisdiction-specific. Observational vaccine-effectiveness estimates should still be interpreted cautiously because healthy-vaccinee/frailty-related selection effects can be large**
- RSV vaccination and cardiopulmonary resilience: **Moderate to Strong product/season-specific observational evidence; a large US claims study found adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccination associated with fewer RSV-related MACE and severe COPD/asthma exacerbation events in adults ≥60 with baseline disease, but residual confounding and current guidance still matter**
- Combined influenza/COVID-19 mRNA vaccines: **Promising/investigational product-specific evidence for durable immunogenicity; require authorization and outcome/surveillance data before treatment-directing use**
- Post-COVID cardiovascular and long-COVID vigilance: **Moderate observational evidence for an early post-infection cardiovascular risk window, especially in older adults; newer target-trial emulation evidence does not show a meaningful third-dose effect on long-COVID incidence, so booster decisions should remain anchored to current severe-disease prevention guidance**
